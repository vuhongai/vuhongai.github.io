*A new class of designer proteins that remove specific antibodies from the blood — opening gene therapy to more patients, and offering a precise new way to treat autoimmune disease.*

---

## The Problem

Antibodies are among the immune system's most powerful weapons — but sometimes they work against us.

- In **gene therapy**, many patients have antibodies that recognize the delivery vector as an intruder and neutralize it before it can act. Depending on the vector, this excludes **30–60% of people** from treatment, and it prevents giving a second dose if one is ever needed.
- In **autoimmune diseases**, the body mistakenly makes antibodies against its own tissues. In **myasthenia gravis**, for example, antibodies attack the connection between nerve and muscle, causing progressive weakness. Roughly 20,000 people live with this disease in France alone.

Today's treatments blunt the whole immune system or strip out *all* antibodies indiscriminately — removing the protective ones along with the harmful ones. **No approved therapy can selectively eliminate just the harmful antibodies** while leaving the rest of the immune defense intact.

---

## My Approach: Send the Bad Antibodies to the Cell's Recycling Center

Every cell has a built-in recycling system — compartments called lysosomes that break down and dispose of unwanted material. My strategy is to build a **two-headed "shuttle" protein**: one end grabs the harmful antibody, the other end carries it to the cell's recycling center, where it is permanently destroyed.

This is fundamentally different from existing drugs. Rather than simply *blocking* an antibody or slowing its production, the shuttle **catches it and eliminates it for good** — and, because we choose what the grabbing end recognizes, we can make it selective.

## Two Directions

**1. Opening gene therapy to more patients.** Our lead shuttle protein, an IgG degrader, rapidly clears antibodies against AAV vectors in laboratory tests and in mice — outperforming an existing clinical enzyme and restoring the vector's ability to work in the presence of blocking antibodies. If this holds up, it could make gene therapy available to patients who are currently excluded, and enable repeat dosing.

**2. Precision treatment for autoimmune disease.** The bigger idea is to make the shuttle *selective*. By replacing the grabbing end with an **AI-designed mini-protein** shaped to catch only the disease-causing antibody, we aim to eliminate the harmful antibodies of myasthenia gravis while sparing the protective ones. If successful, the same blueprint could be adapted to many other antibody-driven autoimmune diseases.

---

## Where It's Going

This is an emerging pillar of my program, developed in collaboration with a leading myasthenia gravis research group at the Institut de Myologie (Paris). A dedicated **doctoral project (SYNPAD-MG, funded by DIM BioConvS)** is now advancing the selective-degradation approach from concept toward preclinical proof.

More broadly, this "grab-and-recycle" strategy is a general-purpose tool: in principle it can be pointed at many harmful proteins in the blood, well beyond antibodies — a versatile new addition to the therapeutic toolbox.
