My research aims to transform therapeutic development from an empirical process into a predictive engineering discipline. At the interface of **artificial intelligence, protein engineering, and gene therapy**, I develop technologies that learn, model, and exploit biological sequence-function relationships to create programmable therapeutic systems.

The overarching goal is to overcome two major barriers that limit the impact of modern biologics and gene therapies: (i) Delivering therapeutic cargos efficiently to the right tissues and (ii) Eliminating pathogenic antibodies that block treatment or drive disease. To address these challenges, I combine large-scale protein diversification, high-throughput functional screening, and machine learning to build predictive models that guide therapeutic design.

My research is organised along two complementary and synergistic axes:

1. **Predictive engineering of AAV vectors** for next-generation gene delivery.
2. **Programmable degradation of pathogenic antibodies** for gene therapy and autoimmune diseases.

Together, these technologies aim to expand patient access, improve therapeutic efficacy, and enable treatments that are currently not achievable.

## Predictive Engineering of AAV Vectors

AAV vectors are the leading platform for in vivo gene delivery, yet their development remains largely dependent on empirical screening. My objective is to establish a predictive framework for AAV engineering, enabling the rational design of vectors with predefined biological properties. To achieve this, I developed an integrated platform that combines:

- simultaneous diversification of multiple capsid variable regions;
- massively parallel measurement of vector performance across millions of variants;
- interpretable deep-learning models that capture complex sequence-function relationships.

Unlike existing approaches, the platform jointly optimises multiple clinically relevant parameters, including tissue tropism, manufacturability, purification compatibility, transgene expression, and resistance to neutralising antibodies (Patent PCT/EP2024/082609). The long-term objective is to establish the design principles required to engineer vectors that are more effective, more manufacturable, and less susceptible to immune barriers.

### Decoding the rules of vector biology

A central challenge in gene therapy is that the molecular determinants governing vector behaviour remain poorly understood. By mapping the relationships between capsid sequence and multiple functional properties at unprecedented scale, we aim to identify the fundamental rules that control biodistribution, cellular entry, manufacturability, and immune recognition. Beyond generating improved vectors, this work seeks to establish a predictive understanding of AAV biology that can accelerate therapeutic development across multiple disease areas.

### Programmable tissue targeting

We apply this framework to engineer vectors tailored to specific therapeutic needs. For neuromuscular and cardiac diseases, we aim to control cardiac-to-skeletal-muscle delivery ratios, enabling vectors whose biodistribution is matched to disease pathology rather than maximised in a single tissue. For durable gene editing, we seek vectors capable of targeting both mature myofibres and muscle stem cells, ensuring long-term correction despite repeated cycles of muscle degeneration and regeneration. For neurological applications, we engineer AAV capsids that exploit transferrin receptor-mediated transcytosis to cross the blood-brain barrier and enable efficient brain delivery. Together, these efforts establish a new paradigm in which vector biodistribution becomes a programmable design parameter rather than a fixed biological constraint.

## Programmable Degradation of Pathogenic Antibodies

Antibodies represent a major limitation across multiple therapeutic areas. In gene therapy, pre-existing neutralising antibodies exclude a large proportion of patients from treatment and prevent vector re-administration. In autoimmune diseases, pathogenic antibodies are themselves the primary drivers of disease. My second research axis focuses on developing programmable protein degraders that induce the selective and irreversible elimination of pathogenic antibodies through lysosomal degradation (Patent EP25306380.4). Unlike existing approaches that transiently reduce antibody levels or broadly suppress immunity, this platform is designed to provide targeted, catalytic, and potentially disease-specific antibody removal.

### Overcoming humoral barriers to gene therapy

We developed bifunctional IgG degraders capable of redirecting circulating antibodies to lysosomes for destruction. Our lead candidate efficiently reduces IgG levels, restores AAV transduction in the presence of neutralising antibodies, and provides a potential strategy to expand patient eligibility and enable repeat dosing of gene therapies. Combined with immune-evasive vector engineering, this approach tackles both sides of the immune barrier: the vector and the host response.

### Precision therapies for autoimmune diseases

We are extending this technology from broad antibody depletion to antigen-specific degradation by incorporating AI-designed epitope-mimetic proteins that selectively capture disease-causing antibodies. As a first application, we are developing degraders targeting anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies in myasthenia gravis. More broadly, this strategy establishes a modular framework for the selective elimination of pathogenic antibodies while preserving protective immunity, potentially creating a new class of precision therapies for antibody-mediated diseases.

## Vision

My long-term vision is to establish AI-enabled therapeutic engineering, where biological function can be predicted from sequence and therapeutics can be designed with predefined properties rather than discovered through trial and error. By integrating machine learning, protein engineering, and translational medicine, I aim to develop programmable systems that improve the precision, durability, and accessibility of gene and biologic therapies, ultimately expanding the range of diseases that can be effectively treated.
