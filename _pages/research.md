Gene therapy can, in principle, correct a disease at its source. In practice, three obstacles still stand in the way: our delivery vehicles are not precise enough, the immune system often blocks the treatment, and we still don't fully understand why some diseases resist it. My research attacks all three — combining **disease biology, protein engineering, and artificial intelligence** into a single pipeline that goes from understanding a disease to building a therapy for it.

My program is organized around three connected themes.

## Why It Fits Together

These themes are not separate projects — they are stages of one idea. Studying **how a disease works** reveals what a therapy must fix. Delivering that fix safely requires **better vectors**. And making those vectors work in every patient means **overcoming the immune barriers** that block them. Each theme feeds the next, and AI runs through all of them as the engine that turns data into design.

The result is a research program that spans the full path from a biological question to a therapeutic candidate — supported by patents, competitive funding, and collaborations across Europe and the US.
